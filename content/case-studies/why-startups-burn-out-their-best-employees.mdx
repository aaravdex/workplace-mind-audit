## Business Problem

A 60-person Series A startup was losing senior engineers at an accelerating rate. Exit interviews blamed "burnout," but leadership saw the same people who had been their highest performers six months earlier. The company was shipping fast, yet velocity was falling and on-call incidents were rising.

## Psychological Explanation

The strongest performers absorb the most demand. Under the Job Demands–Resources model, sustained high demands without matching recovery resources deplete people regardless of motivation — in fact, the most motivated deplete fastest, because they keep saying yes. Low **Capacity** (no recovery) combined with thinning **Connection** (managers too stretched to notice) is the textbook burnout signature.

## Organisational Consequence

Burnout did not announce itself. It showed up as quiet quitting, slipping code quality, and finally resignations clustered among the people the company could least afford to lose. Each departure pulled context and mentoring out of the team, raising the load on whoever remained — a doom loop.

## Behavioural Economics Angle

The culture had accidentally built an incentive to overwork. Late-night messages were praised; visible exhaustion read as commitment. That turned overwork into a **status signal** — and people rationally supply more of whatever earns status. Leadership was rewarding the precise behaviour it claimed to want to stop, then acting surprised when it scaled.

## Recommended Intervention

- Make recovery a visible norm: protected focus time, explicit no-after-hours expectations, and on-call rotations that are actually rotated.
- Stop praising overwork; start recognising outcomes and sustainable pace.
- Give managers the capacity to hold real 1:1s, so strain is caught before it becomes an exit.

## Measurable Outcome

A re-audit one quarter later showed Capacity climbing from the Critical band into Watch, and regretted attrition among senior engineers falling sharply. The fix was not "work less" — it was removing the incentive that made overwork the smart career move.
